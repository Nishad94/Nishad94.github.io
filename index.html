the Gmail Branching Hypothesis 👽

the emails are real; the interpretation is gloriously unserious

Working theory: my inbox is not documenting the timeline. It is testing branches.

Whenever life approaches equilibrium, Gmail seems to produce a portal: university decision, recruiter ping, offer letter, immigration form, relocation thread, or bureaucratic side quest. The hypothesis therefore proposes that Gmail is a Monte Carlo simulator for career and geography.

  1. 2015: FutureMe accidentally supplies the system prompt: fly high, dream big, regain control.
  2. 2016–2018: rejection → retry → CMU. The simulator learns the agent reopens failed paths.
  3. 2021: Tesla and Cruise probes are declined. The simulator learns Nishad does not always maximize novelty.
  4. 2023: multiple career and geography branches collide. Difficulty is increased.
  5. 2024: Microsoft respawns. Apparently some nodes persist in cache.
  6. 2026: stability rises; boredom is reported; recruiter density becomes suspicious again.

In this model, replying “Thanks, interested. Happy to chat.” is functionally equivalent to git checkout -b possible-new-life.

Reality check: this is a joke built from my own career history, not a claim that Gmail, recruiters, or the universe are secretly controlling anything. The multiverse is currently unverified by peer review and my sister.

alternate timelines

the paths not taken, rendered with irresponsible confidence

msft-2020 / Redmond brancharchived
You keep the original Microsoft offer. The pandemic arrives almost immediately. Your twenties become less “Twitter inside the news” and more “Azure from the edge of it.” By 2026, you might be the six-year Microsoft veteran wondering what would have happened if you had taken that Twitter offer.
twilio-2023 / communications branchghost
You take the Twilio branch and stay there. Different coworkers, different city rhythms, different technical problems, different random Friday nights. Eventually the divergence becomes so large that this version of you feels like a cousin with shared childhood memories.
dublin-2025 / Europe branchunmerged
You move to Dublin. The first weeks are logistics, grey skies, unfamiliar streets and novelty. A year later you have favorite cafés, rent complaints, a new social graph, and absolutely no idea what Bengaluru-Nishad is doing at 5 AM.
india-2018 / unopened branchlost history
You do not go to CMU. The US chapter never starts the same way. No Pittsburgh path, no identical professional network, no same sequence of later forks. The tree does not end — it simply grows in a direction you can no longer reconstruct.
